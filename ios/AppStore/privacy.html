Session Planner: Privacy Policy

Effective 23 November 2026

Session Planner is an independent iPhone app that shows your AWS re:Invent schedule as a calendar. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by Amazon Web Services.

What the developer collects

Nothing. The app has no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no server of its own. The developer never receives your sign-in, your schedule, or any information about how you use the app.

Signing in

You sign in on Amazon Web Services' own sign-in page, shown in a private browser sheet. The app never sees your password. When sign-in finishes, AWS gives the app a token that lets it read and change your event schedule. The token is kept in this iPhone's keychain, is not synced to iCloud, and is not copied to any other device or service.

What the app sends, and to whom

The app talks only to the AWS Events service (api.awsevents.com and oauth.awsevents.com). It asks for the session catalog and your schedule, and it sends the changes you make yourself: adding or removing a favorite, reserving a seat, or cancelling a reservation. How AWS handles that information is covered by the AWS Privacy Notice.

What is kept on your iPhone

A copy of the session catalog and your schedule is saved on the phone so the calendar opens without a connection. Three settings are saved as well: whether favorites are shown, whether you are using the sample conference, and whether your reserved sessions are added to Calendar.

Apple Calendar

If you turn on "Add reserved sessions to Calendar" in the app's Settings, the app asks for access to Calendar and writes your reserved sessions into a calendar of their own, named for the conference, on this iPhone. It keeps a small file on the phone listing the events it made, and marks each of those events, so it can update or remove only those: it never changes or deletes any other event. To find one of its events again (for example after you move it to another calendar), the app may look through the events within a day of that session to find its mark; it does not keep or send anything about the others. Calendar data is written on the device only and is not sent to the developer or anyone else; if you use iCloud Calendar, Apple syncs that calendar like any other. Turning the setting off stops the updates; delete the calendar in the Calendar app to remove the events.

Removing your information

Choose Settings, then Sign out, in the app. This asks AWS to revoke the token, removes it from the keychain, and deletes the saved calendar. Your schedule on the conference site is not changed. Signing out also turns off adding sessions to Calendar and removes the app's list of the events it made; the events themselves stay until you delete their calendar in the Calendar app. Sign out before deleting the app: iOS can keep keychain items after an app is deleted.

The sample conference

The sample conference is made-up data stored inside the app. Using it sends nothing anywhere.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no information from anyone.
